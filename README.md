Abstract

Background: Lung cancer accounts for an estimated 1.8 million fatalities annually, with non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) contributing 85% of cases. While artificial intelligence (AI) has advanced prognostic modeling, current approaches face a crucial translational barrier, for instance, deep learning achieves high predictive performance but operates as interpretability-limited "black boxes". In addition, mechanistic models require computationally intensive Monte Carlo parameter fitting. Hence, this gap between prediction accuracy and clinical interpretability impedes adoption in precision oncology workflows.

Methodology: We introduce 3D-MLPA, a three-dimensional Multi-scale Parameterized Automata – a framework modeling for human NSCLC. Our approach utilizes a novel four-stage pipeline: (1) extraction of biologically-grounded radiomic features (intensity entropy reflecting tumor heterogeneity; sphericity capturing invasive morphology); (2) deterministic mapping to mechanistic parameters (proliferation rate α, necrosis probability β) grounded in established radiogenomic associations; (3) patient-specific spatiotemporal simulation on anatomically-constrained voxel lattices representing individualized lung architecture; and (4) derivation of emergent temporal biomarkers (growth dynamics, necrotic burden) that encode disease trajectory information inaccessible to static imaging analysis.

Results: This approach was evaluated on the NSCLC-Radiomics benchmark (n=422, stratified 5-fold cross-validation), 3D-MLPA achieved C-index 0.612±0.026, statistically comparable to state-of-the-art deep learning (0.630). It also demonstrated substantially increase in time-dependent discrimination (integrated AUC: 0.667±0.033 versus 0.591, +12.8% improvement, p<0.05). Moreover, ablation analysis confirms that simulation-derived temporal features provide statistically significant complementary prognostic information beyond static radiomics (+3.4%, p<0.05), verifying the significance of mechanistic modeling.

Clinical Impact: Unlike black-box alternatives, 3D-MLPA enables clinicians to investigate patient-specific risk mechanisms, such as rapid proliferation (high α, potentially targetable with cell-cycle inhibitors) versus extensive necrosis (high β, indicating hypoxic microenvironment). This mechanistic transparency tackles a fundamental barrier to clinical AI adoption: explaining why a patient is high-risk, rather than just stating that they are.

Conclusion: Overall, 3D-MLPA reveals that digital twin technology can bridge the accuracy-interpretability gap in cancer, achieving competitive predictive performance while offering the mechanistic insights required for precision medicine decision-making. 

Future work: While our aim is a full-scale of patient-specific tumor evolution, the present framework represents an initial step. Future work will integrate biochemical feedback loops, such as incorporating genomic alterations (e.g., EGFR or KRAS status) into tumor growth dynamics, to enable multi-scale, personalized modeling.
